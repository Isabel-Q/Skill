# Editorial standard

## Purpose

Create notes that improve future judgment. A successful note lets the reader recover the article's useful argument, locate it by search, understand what is questionable, and reuse the insight in another market or project.

## Content hierarchy

### Market cognition

Extract only specific mechanisms, such as:

- where value and bargaining power move;
- what changes the industry's unit economics;
- which moat strengthens or weakens;
- how pricing units and contract structures change;
- what second-order effects appear in suppliers, labor, distribution or capital;
- which incentives prevent incumbents from self-disruption.

### Consumer and demand insight

Look for:

- effective paying market versus headline population;
- willingness to pay, frequency, retention and switching costs;
- distribution and customer-acquisition constraints;
- enterprise procurement, adoption, trust and compliance barriers;
- how local customer habits shape what suppliers learn to build.

### Methodology

Turn reasoning into reusable tools:

- a checklist with observable criteria;
- a causal chain with assumptions;
- a metric table stating what each metric reveals;
- a comparison frame separating demand, supply, economics and regulation;
- leading indicators versus lagging indicators.

### Know-how

Prefer executable detail:

- what to check each quarter;
- which primary documents to open;
- what denominator or comparison period is required;
- how to rule out alternative explanations;
- how to tell a renamed old business from genuine new revenue.

## Remove empty language

Delete sentences that merely say change is fast, innovation matters, users are important, companies must transform, or opportunities coexist with challenges. Keep such a sentence only when it names a mechanism, decision, metric or consequence.

## Evidence and causality

Apply these tests:

1. **Definition:** Is the metric defined? Does “market size” mean revenue, spending, GMV, profit or valuation?
2. **Denominator:** Does a percentage state what it is a share of?
3. **Time window:** Are start date, end date and comparison basis stated?
4. **Apples to apples:** Are currencies, constant-currency growth, fiscal years and calendar years aligned?
5. **Source:** Can the number be traced to an original document?
6. **Selection:** Does a benchmark cover representative tasks or a narrow subset?
7. **Quality adjustment:** Does a speed or cost claim control for output quality and failure rate?
8. **Alternative causes:** Could macro conditions, valuation, exchange rates, regulation, customer budgets or company-specific events explain the outcome?
9. **Correlation versus causation:** Did two events merely occur near each other?
10. **Extrapolation:** Is a company or task result being generalized to an industry, labor force or country?

## Annotation levels

Use Obsidian callouts consistently:

- `[!danger]` for a material contradiction with a primary source or a claim that would reverse the conclusion.
- `[!warning]` for unverifiable numbers, missing definitions, misleading extrapolation or weak causality.
- `[!info]` for context, nuance or a basically correct claim requiring qualification.

State what the article said, what the evidence says, and how the conclusion should change.

## Credibility property

Use one short calibrated label:

- `整体可靠`
- `核心逻辑可靠，部分数字需核验`
- `核心逻辑有价值，部分数据与因果表述需校正`
- `争议较大，仅作观点样本`
- `关键结论不可靠`

## Searchability standard

The main Markdown note must contain:

- full article title and useful aliases;
- people, companies, products, countries and industries central to the argument;
- critical numbers and their units;
- distinctive terms a reader might remember later;
- the article's causal chain in plain text;
- tags for topic and insight type.

Do not create a duplicate transcript solely for search. Expand the `文章有效内容` section until it carries enough searchable substance.

## Quality bar

Before finalizing, ask:

- Would the insight still be useful with the article title hidden?
- Can a concrete decision or investigation be derived from it?
- Are the sharpest claims checked rather than repeated?
- Is the commentary distinguishable from the author's argument?
- Could the user find this note six months later from a company, number or concept they remember?
